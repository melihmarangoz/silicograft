Methods notes

Model. A 30 mm empty defect in a sheep tibia, fixed with a plate, followed for 360 days. A cell agent model coupled to a CalculiX finite element solution, with Claes-Heigele mechanoregulation.

Data. The simulation was re-run to save the whole lattice every day at 0.1 mm resolution. A lattice site holds at most one cell agent. A region is 2.8 × 2.8 mm and 0.5 mm thick. The four presets lie in the mid-frontal plane; on the section map any other box can be opened anywhere along the bone and toward or away from the plate, up to about 1.8 mm off that plane. Every agent inside a region is drawn as one cell at its position in the model.

Difference from the earlier viewer. These runs repeat the runs of the 10-day voxel viewer with the same seed and settings, but they did not come out identical. With the unbonded plate, tissue volumes agree within 1% for the first 6 months, and bone volume differs by up to 10% on day 359. With the bonded plate the difference starts earlier and reaches 29% in bone on day 359. The non-union and the tissue band along the plate are the same in both versions.

Scale. Cells are drawn at 1.5 times their true size, the same factor for every type. The spacing between cells comes from the model's 100 µm lattice. Real tissue packs cells more densely.

Bone cells. The model has a single bone cell type. A bone agent with at least five non-bone sites among its 26 neighbours counts as being on the surface and is drawn as an osteoblast, its base toward the bone and its nucleus on the far side. Bone agents inside the bone are drawn as osteocytes.

Placement. Each cell stays inside its own 100 µm lattice site. It is shifted by up to 35 µm and its size varied within 10% so that the lattice rows do not show. The inside of the intact cortex is a cell-free boundary in the model, so only its tissue is drawn there. The horizontal MSC bands in the marrow canal come from the model: at the start, stem cells are seeded in every third lattice layer of the marrow.

Motion. Only stem cells migrate in the model (at most 7 lattice steps per day). The model does not record cell identity, so the path between two days is drawn by nearest matching and is for display only. Division, differentiation and loss are read from the difference between two daily records.

Matrix. Bone, cartilage and fibrous tissue surfaces are drawn from smoothed agent density. Empty lattice sites are shown as clot and granulation tissue, a reddish gel with thin fibrin threads. The model has no vessels and no hypertrophic chondrocytes, so neither is drawn.

Comparison. The "Compare" menu in the top bar opens a second run on the right. Both views show the same region, the same day and the same camera, and the colour range covers both runs. Cell counts, the slice and the table come from the left run, and the chart draws the right run dashed. Even repeats with the same settings did not come out identical (the difference above), so do not put small differences between two runs down to the plate or the stimulus sign alone.

Slice and table. The Slice panel shows a single plane of the day's lattice: each square is one lattice site (100 µm), and the lines are borders between types. Here the cells sit at the model's lattice sites, without the small offsets of the 3D view or the estimated path between two days. The Exact surface option runs the tissue surface halfway between two lattice sites, without smoothing. Where the faces of the box cut through cells, the cut face is drawn approximately, as an ellipsoid fitted to the cell body.
