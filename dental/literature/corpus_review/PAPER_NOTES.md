# Batch 01 extraction notes (28 papers)

### doi:10.5505/eudfd.2019.26213
- RCT (Gürlek & Sönmez 2019, Turkish text with English abstract): 28 immediate implants in maxillary premolars, ~2 mm buccal gap filled with β-TCP (SynthoGraft, Bicon); custom vs standard healing abutment; CBCT (Kodak 9000) at 1 wk and 6 mo.
- The only paper in this batch that matches our exact scenario (immediate implant + pure β-TCP jumping gap) with serial CBCT, but buccal bone thickness is shown only in graphs (Grafik 1-3). No numbers can be extracted.
- Caveat: the abstract says "premolar"; the methods say maxillary premolars. Bone and β-TCP are measured as one radiopaque layer, so the graft cannot be followed separately.

### doi:10.1186/s12903-026-07683-2
- Preliminary RCT (n=20, maxillary esthetic zone, type II sockets): immediate implant with EthOss putty (65% β-TCP / 35% CaSO4) in the labial gap, collagen vs Alb-PRF membrane; CBCT at baseline, 6 and 12 mo with MBL and labial bone thickness (Table 1).
- Close to our scenario and gives a 12-month CBCT series per arm, so it is good validation data for labial wall evolution with a β-TCP-based filler.
- Caveats: not pure β-TCP (CaSO4 fraction resorbs fast). LBT levels at 3/6/9 mm are defined, but only one value per time point is reported. The text is inconsistent on which group kept more LBT, and baseline timing is ambiguous (pre-surgical vs immediate post-op). Author names are absent from the text file.

### doi:10.1186/s40729-024-00533-2
- Retrospective cohort (Kuebler & Noelken 2024): 60 flapless immediate implants in sites with total buccal wall loss, gap grafted with autogenous bone chips ± CTG, CB-CT pre-op and at 1-13 yr (mean 60.8 mo).
- Gives long-term buccal wall thickness at 1/3/6 mm (about 1.7-1.9 mm) regenerated from zero, which is a useful end-state target for a long simulation. Graft is autograft, not β-TCP.
- Caveat: single private practice, follow-up length differs per site, so time is not a clean variable. Data on request.

### doi:10.1002/jper.11381
- Secondary analysis of an RCT (Jung et al. 2026): 45 immediate implants (maxillary anterior/premolar) with bovine bone in the gap, ± SCTG/ADM; pre-op vs 6-mo CBCT superimposition with volumetric buccal bone loss in coronal, middle and apical 3-mm segments (Table 2).
- One of the most useful validation sets: zone-wise relative loss (coronal ~26-28%, middle/apical ~10-12%, total ~16.5%) plus gap size (HDD ~2.2-2.5 mm) and baseline buccal plate thickness (~0.65 mm).
- Caveats: xenograft not β-TCP. Baseline ROI includes tooth structure. Graft and bone are not separated. Two follow-up scans came from another CBCT unit.

### doi:10.25241/stomaeduj.2019.6(1).art.5
- Retrospective series (Ionescu 2019): 42 immediate tissue-level implants with open healing, gaps filled mostly with Bio-Oss (some Copios, PRGF), CBCT up to 4 yr after loading; buccal/oral plate heights in Table 4.
- Long follow-up but only plate heights (no thickness), graft arms pooled, mixed sites including molar septa. Weak validation value.
- Caveat: text says CBCT at 24 and 48 mo while Table 4 says "after loading / 1 yr / 4 yr after loading"; crest width named but not reported.

### doi:10.1111/cid.12701
- Cohort (Groningen group, 2019; authors not in the text file): immediate implants in the maxillary esthetic zone with large (≥2 mm) vs no/small buccal defects; tuberosity block + autologous/Bio-Oss (0.25-1.0 mm) fill; CBCT pre-extraction, ~7 mo and ~18 mo; buccal thickness at M0-M5 (Table 1).
- Useful: shows the gap-grafted buccal layer stabilizes at ~1.1-1.5 mm from 7 to 18 mo regardless of initial defect, and the original thin wall is resorbed in the control group (~0.5-1 mm loss).
- Caveats: subjects come from two earlier Slagter RCTs (duplicate cohort). Funded by Nobel Biocare. The authors note the radiopaque layer may not be bone.

### doi:10.7759/cureus.32121
- RCT (Tomar 2022): 90 immediate implants with PRP vs PerioGlas vs Bio-Oss gap graft, CBCT at baseline, 3, 6, 12 mo; mesial/distal "bone resorption" in Table 4.
- Low value for us: the reported "resorption" values drop from ~5 mm at 3 mo to ~3 mm at 12 mo, so they cannot be cumulative loss. The measure is ill-defined.
- Caveat: PerioGlas is bioactive glass but is called a "synthetic allograft"; labial width was measured but not reported; site distribution not given.

### doi:10.4317/medoral.28158
- RCT (Sáez-Alcaide 2026): 40 thin-phenotype (<1.5 mm) sites, socket shield without graft vs conventional immediate implant with bovine xenograft in a ≥2 mm buccal gap; pre-op and 1-yr CBCT superimposition (Table 2), MBL on periapicals.
- The control arm is a clean "IIP + particulate gap graft, thin wall" reference: buccal width loss ~0.7-0.9 mm, buccal height loss ~1.0 mm, ~35.5 mm3 volume loss at 1 yr, with baseline wall thickness (0.70-1.01 mm at 1-5 mm).
- Caveat: arms differ in grafting as well as in technique; SDs in Table 2 look small relative to the reported percentages.

### doi:10.6026/973206300223702
- Prospective study (Bioinformation 2026): 30 immediate implants with CBCT at baseline, immediately post-op and 6 mo.
- Not usable: the methods describe statistics only (no surgical protocol, graft, device), and Tables 2-5 are missing from the text file. Only r=0.46 (primary stability vs buccal thickness preservation) is extractable.
- Caveat: very low reporting quality; treat as unreliable.

### doi:10.36065/robrac.v26i76.1016
- Case report (Gomes 2017): immediate implants at #11 and #21, provisional made from the patient's own crowns, bovine Bonefill in a >2 mm labial gap, CBCT at 30, 60, 90 d (Fig 1).
- Only qualitative serial CBCT; useful at most as an illustration. ISQ 57 with immediate non-occlusal provisionalization.
- Caveat: graft described as bovine in the text but "lyophilized bone" in a figure caption; screening listed day 120, the text shows 30-90 d.

### doi:10.3389/fcimb.2025.1555964
- Retrospective cohort (2025): 234 immediate implants in periodontitis patients (stage II-IV), Bio-Oss/Bio-Gide as needed; 12-mo MBL on CBCT by stage and grade (Table 5); one case with CBCT at 0, 3, 6 mo (Fig 5).
- Marginal relevance: shows MBL rises with periodontitis stage (0.63 to 0.97 mm at 12 mo). Graft use per implant unknown; no buccal-wall data.
- Caveat: author names only as initials in the text file.

### doi:10.1016/j.jds.2025.07.3456
- Pilot RCT (J Dent Sci 2026): 20 immediate implants after anterior trauma, Bio-Oss Collagen in a >2 mm labial gap; non-loaded native-crown/FRC-splint provisional vs delayed restoration; CBCT immediately and at 4-5 mo.
- Useful number: labial plate (bone + graft) thins by ~1.0-1.2 mm within 4-5 months at the neck and 2 mm below, independent of the provisional. Also gives alveolar bone CT value 548±83 HU.
- Caveat: graft not β-TCP; short follow-up; authors not in the text file.

### doi:10.14232/phd.10151
- PhD thesis (Gluckman 2019, Szeged) compiling seven published papers on partial extraction therapy (socket shield, pontic shield, root submergence) with case CBCTs and a 128-case survival series.
- No quantitative imaging. Useful cited background: 90% of anterior maxillary sites have facial wall ≤1 mm; human socket-healing timeline (clot → granulation tissue by wk 1 → provisional matrix wk 2-4 → woven bone wk 6-8 → mature bone ~24 wk); the author recommends a fast-resorbing filler so that late CBCT density reflects bone.
- Caveat: duplicate of published papers; all parameter values are secondary (cited).

### doi:10.1111/cid.12628
- RCT (Lee, Cha & Kim 2018): 94 damaged (periodontitis) sockets, mostly molars, grafted with DBBM vs porcine DPBM (both 0.25-1.0 mm) + collagen membrane; CT immediately and at 4 mo, superimposed (Table 2).
- Gives zone-wise (apical/middle/coronal) horizontal shrinkage of a xenograft-filled socket (coronal ~2.1-2.7 mm) and volume loss (~6-8%, very wide SD). Useful as a contrast for slowly resorbing grafts.
- Caveat: not immediate implant, not β-TCP; the CT device and voxel are not reported; histology is in a separate paper.

### doi:10.1186/s40729-023-00491-1
- RCT (2023, ACTA/Shanghai): socket preservation with β-TCP (0.25-1 mm) vs rhBMP-2/BioCaP/β-TCP vs empty socket; CBCT grey values at 0 and 6 wk and a 6-wk biopsy at the GV point (Tables 3-6).
- High value for β-TCP early kinetics: at 6 wk, residual β-TCP area 20.6±9.5% and new bone 13.4±6.0% (β-TCP alone); empty sockets 9.5% new bone. GV falls in grafted sockets and rises in empty sockets.
- Caveats: rhBMP-2 dose not given; the natural-healing SD 0.08 looks like a typo; biopsies taken freehand; the text file has only two lines but is complete. First author known only as "YS".

### doi:10.4172/2161-1122.1000505
- Case series (Wei 2018): 3 periodontitis molar sockets preserved with DBBM + Bio-Gide, implants at 6 mo, CBCT at extraction, 6 mo and 30 mo after loading.
- Gives 6-mo histology of DBBM-grafted molar sockets (new bone 11.5%, connective tissue 53.4%, residual graft 35.0%), useful as a slow-resorbing comparator. n=3, so weak.
- Caveat: the residual-graft range printed (35.84-50.87%) does not contain the mean (35.04%); baseline ridge widths of 1.4-1.9 mm look like residual-wall measurements.

### doi:10.54133/ajms.v8i1.1413
- Split-mouth RCT (Mohammed & Al-Jumaily 2025): 32 sockets preserved with bovine xenograft ± local PTH(1-34); CBCT at baseline and 4 mo; ISQ at implant placement.
- Marginal relevance: small height/width changes (0.4-0.75 mm) and a PTH dose (2.0 µl of 0.40 mg/ml per 157 mm3 graft).
- Caveat: baseline described both as "before extraction" and "after extraction"; BBH and LBH are both described as buccal/labial heights; maxillary and mandibular sites mixed.

### file:Okada_2016_IntJImplantDent
- Prospective study (Okada 2016, Int J Implant Dent, doi 10.1186/s40729-016-0042-6): 30 lateral sinus lifts with pure β-TCP (OSferion) and 58 simultaneous implants; CBCT pre-op, immediately, 6 mo and ~2.5 yr (Tables 5-6).
- Key β-TCP resorption curve in humans: graft volume falls to 75.6% at 6 mo and 54.9% at 2.5 yr; graft above the implant tip falls from 2.0 mm to −0.7 mm. β-TCP is distinguishable from bone on CBCT at 6 mo but not at 2.5 yr.
- Caveats: volume units are printed as cm3 but the values only make sense as mm3; sinus pneumatization drives part of the loss (not the same biology as a jumping gap); the follow-up time of the last scan varies; β-TCP was donated by the manufacturer.

### doi:10.3390/biomimetics9050276
- Pilot study (Valencia 2024): 13 transcrestal sinus lifts with osseodensification and a putty graft plus simultaneous implants; CBCT T0, T1 (immediate), T2 (12 mo).
- Gives a graft height loss of ~0.9 mm over 12 months and apical gain falling from 2.3 to 1.3 mm.
- Caveats: the graft (NovaBone Dental Putty) is called β-TCP in the text but is a calcium phosphosilicate product, so it should not be used as β-TCP data; SDs differ between text and Tables 3-4; surgeries done by master's students.

### doi:10.1186/s40729-022-00456-w
- Retrospective comparative study (De Angelis 2022): 45 single extractions, ARP with bovine granules (0.25-1 mm) + pericardium membrane vs spontaneous healing; CBCT 1 wk before and 6 mo after extraction.
- Useful socket-healing baseline: spontaneous horizontal loss 2.03/1.35/0.94 mm at 1/3/5 mm vs 0.86/0.55/0.46 mm with ARP.
- Caveats: not randomized (patients chose); the "volume" values come from a method described for STL soft-tissue models, so they are likely not bone volume.

### doi:10.1016/j.jpor.2018.07.003
- Prospective study (Kato 2018): 34 anterior maxillary implants (delayed, site development, immediate with Bio-Oss gap + CTG); CBCT at prosthesis delivery and 1 yr later (Table 2).
- Low value for healing: imaging starts only at loading, after the gap has healed. Buccal bone thickness in the immediate group stays ~1.5-1.7 mm over the first loaded year.
- Caveat: CBCT device not reported; medians with IQR only.

### doi:10.3390/ma17092136
- Rabbit study (Hemmerlein 2024): 3D-printed Ca-Mg phosphate (CMPC) and Mg phosphate (MPC) wedges in a partially loaded tibial defect, in vivo micro-CT every 2-4 wk up to 30 wk plus histology at 6, 12, 24, 30 wk.
- Useful as an analog of a resorbable CaP under partial load: ~71-72% volume lost by wk 6 and ~98-100% by wk 16; woven bone with cartilage at 6 wk, lamellar by 24 wk; cell-rich resorption zone 1.1-1.3 mm at 6 wk. Also gives compressive strength (15.6 and 21.5 MPa) and porosity.
- Caveat: these are Mg-containing cements that resorb much faster than β-TCP; do not use the rates as β-TCP rates.

### doi:10.1111/jcpe.13463
- Split-mouth RCT (Castro 2021): 60 premaxillary sockets with L-PRF vs A-PRF+ vs unassisted healing; CBCT immediately and at 3 mo (0.2 mm voxel); micro-CT and histology of biopsies from 5 patients.
- Good ungrafted early-healing reference: buccal crest width loss ~1.6-1.7 mm (~26-28%) at 3 mo, socket fill 68% (empty) vs 84-85% (PRF), histologic BV/TV 34.7% (empty) vs 47.7-54.5% (PRF).
- Caveats: text is the accepted-manuscript version; KU Leuven holds an Intra-Lock research chair that partly funded the work; the palatal control value sign looks inconsistent.

### doi:10.3390/coatings8010017
- Dog study (Lee 2018, Coatings): heparinized Ti implants carrying rhPDGF-BB and/or rhBMP-2 in 2.5-mm supra-alveolar buccal defects of healed ridges; in vitro release over 28 d; ex vivo micro-CT and histometry at 8 wk.
- Useful only for growth-factor parameters: release of ~15-27 ng on day 1 and ~35-70 ng by day 28 per disc; BMP-2 raises defect bone volume from ~18% to ~41-44% at 8 wk; no added effect of PDGF-BB.
- Caveat: no serial imaging (single terminal micro-CT); not a socket model; the text file contains the article twice (duplicated extraction).

### doi:10.1111/cid.12259
- Dog study (Xu 2015): immediate implants in mandibular premolar sockets with standardized mesial defects (6×4×5 mm) filled with β-TCP (GEM 21S, 250-1000 µm) ± rhPDGF-BB ± autologous BMSCs; fluorochrome labels at 3/6/9 wk, histology at 12 wk.
- Directly relevant to the β-TCP peri-implant gap: β-TCP alone gives 19.1% new bone and 32% BIC in the grafted zone at 12 wk; adding PDGF-BB and BMSCs raises this to 48.7% and 72.5%. Gives a PDGF-BB loading dose (0.3 mg/mL) and BMSC density (2×10^7 cells/mL).
- Caveats: no CT; residual β-TCP not quantified; "1" in the text stands for "±"; publication year in text is 2015 (early view).

### doi:10.1155/2019/2797210
- Prospective case-control study (Beltrán 2019): 13 grafted (easy-graft CLASSIC, β-TCP) vs 13 ungrafted anterior maxillary sockets, endoscopic analysis of the implant drill hole at 4-6 mo.
- Gives residual bone substitute area 18.2% at 4-6 mo, with higher vessel counts in grafted sites. Useful as a single late β-TCP residual point.
- Caveats: endoscopic surface area, not histology. The abstract's ± values are SE (Table SD of BSA is 18.72). Ungrafted vessel area is 1.18% in the abstract but 1.82% in Table 3. The commercial product is polymer-coated β-TCP; the text file does not state the composition.

### doi:10.3390/dj13100469
- Secondary analysis (Botticelli 2025): re-reports histomorphometry from four rabbit sinus-lift studies (autograft, Bio-Oss, Bio-Oss Collagen, HA/β-TCP 60:40 granules and paste, Gen-Os) and computes a "break-even" day when new bone equals residual graft.
- Useful kinetic numbers for CaP grafts: HA/β-TCP granules 2.65→34.2% bone and 52.05→37.38% graft between 2 and 10 wk (break-even ~74 d); autograft break-even 18 d; Bio-Oss 82 d or never.
- Caveats: secondary data (not primary measurements); HA/β-TCP is biphasic, not pure β-TCP; HA/β-TCP break-even values are extrapolated beyond the last time point; the Table 2 column labelled "BIC%" is actually area %.

### doi:10.1016/j.reth.2024.08.019
- Mouse study (Regen Ther 2024): immediate HA/β-TCP-blasted Ti implants in maxillary first-molar sockets ± L-PRP, histology at 3, 5, 7, 14, 28 d.
- Gives a dated early-healing sequence (no bone at d3, revascularization and first matrix at d5, contact and distance osteogenesis at d7, mature bone by d14-28) and L-PRP platelet enrichment (1.6-9.4×).
- Caveats: mouse timescale is much faster than human; osseointegration and proliferation rates are graph only; author names absent from the text file.

# Extraction notes, batch 02

### doi:10.4236/ojst.2024.1411035
- Sharroud 2024 (Open J Stomatol): RCT, 24 men, immediate implants in posterior mandibular (molar) sockets, jumping gap filled with β-TCP putty vs β-TCP granules, CBCT immediately and at 6 mo.
- Our exact scenario (immediate implant + pure β-TCP gap). The only serial-volume numbers: grafted volume fell 14.5% (putty) and 27.9% (granules) in 6 mo, so granule beds shrink about twice as fast. Usable as a coarse resorption/shrinkage target.
- Caveats: product, granule size, gap width and CBCT device not stated. Volume is printed in "mm" and the "bone amount %" (30 vs 17.6%) has no method, both reported only in the Discussion. Gap is called "defective sockets" in Methods while socket wall defects were an exclusion criterion. Small low-tier journal. Treat as weak validation data.

### doi:10.1055/s-0045-1801849
- Retrospective (text also says "prospective") single-arm multicentre registry (UK, Dubai), 31 immediate implants in maxillary incisor/canine sockets, circumferential jumping gap filled with EthOss (65% β-TCP / 35% CaSO4), immediate non-occlusal provisionals; CBCT before ITRT and at >=12 mo after loading.
- Close to our scenario but the graft is not pure β-TCP. Single number pair: labial bone thickness 0.7 mm at placement to 2.3 mm at >=12 mo (+1.7 mm), with ranges, no SD. Useful as a rough end-state target for the buccal side of a grafted gap.
- Caveats: author names not in the text file; probable industry link (implant and graft both promoted as "novel", no COI statement in the text); the cited 12-wk histology claims (>50% bone, 10-12% residual) come from other studies; CBCT device, voxel and gap width not given.

### doi:10.34172/japid.026.4099
- Shirmohammadi 2026 (J Adv Periodontol Implant Dent): RCT, 22 maxillary anterior immediate implants with >2 mm buccal gap, filled with autogenous dentin (1-1.5 mm particles) or autogenous bone, plus CTG in both; CBCT pre-op and 6 mo.
- Close scenario (immediate implant, grafted gap) but no β-TCP. Gives pre-extraction buccal wall thickness in the anterior maxilla (0.57-0.80 mm at 2 and 5 mm below crest) and 6-mo buccal thickness (1.8 mm at 2 mm, 2.1-3.0 mm at 5 mm). The level-wise pattern (more gain apically) is a useful zone-wise target.
- Caveats: Results text and Table 3 disagree for the dentin 5-mm value (2.64 vs 2.07 mm) and SDs; Table 4 SD is a copy of the mean. CBCT device and voxel not given; implant system not given. Small n.

### doi:10.1002/jper.20-0845
- Slagter 2021 (J Periodontol, Groningen): 5-yr RCT, 40 patients with >=5 mm labial bony defects in the maxillary esthetic zone; immediate NobelActive implant + tuberosity bone block + autogenous/Bio-Oss mix vs ARP and implant at 3 mo; CBCT pre-extraction, 1 mo and 5 yr after crown, buccal bone thickness at M0-M5 (neck to 5 mm apical).
- Not β-TCP, but a long, level-wise buccal bone thickness record around grafted immediate implants: ~0.9-1.25 mm at ~7 mo rising to ~1.2-1.6 mm at 5 yr. Usable as a long-term end-state target for the buccal wall in a grafted defect site.
- Caveats: the authors say the 1 mo -> 5 yr thickness increase may reflect graft maturation that makes bone more visible on CBCT, not true gain. Medians/IQR only. CBCT parameters only by reference (Maes et al.). Same cohort as the 10-yr paper doi:10.1002/jper.24-0251. Nobel Biocare funded.

### doi:10.5772/intechopen.79871
- Um et al., IntechOpen chapter (2018): case-based review of demineralized dentin matrix (DDM) loaded with rhBMP-2, including immediate implants with the gap filled with DDM/rhBMP-2 powder (300-800 µm) and no membrane; CBCT images at 0, 3, 6 mo and 3 y 7 mo for one molar case.
- Not β-TCP and no measurements. Useful only for the rhBMP-2 dose on a particulate carrier (5.0 µg of 0.2 mg/mL per 0.3 g powder, wording ambiguous) and as qualitative CBCT sequence (thin buccal cortex thins at 3 mo, mostly gone at 6 mo, mature bone at 3.6 y).
- Caveat: first author belongs to Korea Tooth Bank R&D, the DDM maker, though the chapter declares no conflict. Cases are hand-picked. Table 1 demography is missing from the text file.

### doi:10.1186/s12903-021-01752-4
- Dai 2021 (BMC Oral Health): retrospective, 70 patients / 80 immediate implants in the maxillary esthetic zone, residual gap grafted with mineralized collagen (nano-HA/collagen, Allgens) or Bio-Oss; CBCT (Planmeca ProMax 3D) post-op, 6 and 12 mo after the crown.
- Close scenario, but neither graft is β-TCP. Gives labial crest change at the implant shoulder: ~0.7 mm horizontal and ~1.6 mm vertical loss by ~12 mo, no difference between a slowly resorbing xenograft and a CaP/collagen composite. A plausible order of magnitude for crestal remodelling of a grafted gap.
- Caveats: only deltas reported, no absolute thickness; 12-mo-after-crown losses are printed smaller than 6-mo losses; membrane use and gap width not stated; mineralized collagen is HA-based, not β-TCP.

### doi:10.1186/s12903-023-03345-9
- Hamed 2023 (BMC Oral Health): RCT, 24 maxillary anterior class II (labial dehiscence) sockets, immediate implant by vestibular socket therapy, gap/defect filled with collagen plug, DBM Grafton or autogenous chips + DBBM under a 1-mm equine lamina; CBCT pre-op, 6 and 12 mo.
- Close scenario without β-TCP. Pre-op facial wall is very thin (median 0.2-0.35 mm); facial bone thickness reaches ~1.0-1.4 mm by 12 mo. Shows that even a non-mineral filler (collagen plug) under a barrier gives ~1 mm facial bone: a useful "empty-gap-with-barrier" contrast for the model.
- Caveat: the abstract's means (e.g. 2.31±0.86 mm at 12 mo) do not fit the medians and ranges in Table 3; use Table 3. The measurement level is not defined. CBCT device not given. Early loading at ~2 mo.

### doi:10.1007/s10266-025-01271-6
- Mohamad 2026 (Odontology): RCT, 35 patients / 40 immediate implants in mandibular premolar and molar sockets, gap filled with bovine xenograft, covered by an autogenous dentin membrane or a collagen membrane; CBCT (Planmeca ProMax 3D Max) at 0, 3 (loading) and 9 mo.
- Mandibular posterior immediate implants, as in our model, but xenograft. Useful time course: peri-implant CBCT grey value rises 614 -> 822 -> 993 HU (0/3/9 mo) and buccal/lingual crest loss of ~0.7 mm at 3 mo and ~1.3-1.4 mm at 9 mo. The density curve is a candidate qualitative target for mineralizing bone in the gap.
- Caveats: CBCT HU are uncalibrated; HU sites include graft. Membrane comparison has no effect, so both arms can be pooled. Gap width not reported.

### doi:10.3390/jcm12082783
- J Clin Med 2023 (Pisa group; author names not in the text file): retrospective, 10 immediate implants in maxillary molar/premolar sockets with chronic lesions but intact walls, gap filled only with gelatin sponge, PEEK custom healing abutment; CBCT (Gendex GXCB-500, 300 µm voxel) pre-op, 4 and 12 mo, superimposed.
- An ungrafted posterior gap control: crest width 8.94 -> 8.77 (4 mo) -> 8.96 mm (12 mo), so practically no net width loss with a sealed socket. Useful as the "no graft" arm in validation and as a reminder that wide posterior sockets lose little width.
- Caveats: n=10, maxillary not mandibular; only one width level. Infected sockets, which differs from our healthy-socket scenario. No raw data despite DICOM-based superimposition.

### doi:10.1002/jper.24-0251
- Meijer 2025 (J Periodontol): 10-yr follow-up of the same Groningen RCT as doi:10.1002/jper.20-0845 (immediate NobelActive implant + tuberosity bone + Bio-Oss in labial-defect sockets vs ARP + delayed implant). CBCT pre-op, 1 mo and 10 yr after crown; buccal bone thickness at M0-M5.
- The longest serial-CBCT record in this batch: buccal bone at 10 yr is ~1.2-1.6 mm (Immediate) and ~1.0-1.5 mm (Delayed), similar to or above the 1-mo values. Good long-horizon end-state target for a grafted buccal wall; marginal bone loss stays under 1 mm.
- Caveats: duplicate cohort (do not count twice with the 5-yr paper); median/IQR only; graft is autogenous + Bio-Oss, not β-TCP; Nobel Biocare grant. Raw data on request.

### doi:10.11607/jomi.3707
- Siormpas 2014 (IJOMI): retrospective case series, 46 immediate implants in the maxillary anterior with the "root-membrane" (socket-shield) technique, buccal root fragment retained, no graft, immediate non-functional provisionals; follow-up 24-60 mo.
- Not our scenario: no jumping gap graft and the buccal bundle bone is preserved by the root fragment. CBCT pairs exist for only 4 patients and are qualitative. Only number: crestal bone loss ~0.2 mm on periapicals.
- Caveat: notes that the radiolucent line at the implant-root interface on CBCT may be a titanium streak artefact, a point worth remembering when reading peri-implant CBCT.

### doi:10.53730/ijhs.v6ns2.6909
- Arora 2022 (Int J Health Sci): single case, two immediate NobelActive implants in maxillary central incisor sockets with absent/partial labial wall, GBR with autogenous bone + Bio-Oss under Bio-Gide; CBCT pre-op and 36 mo after loading.
- Weak relevance: one case, xenograft, no immediate post-op scan. Only numbers: pre-op partial labial plate 0.8 mm and labial wall 2.0-2.5 mm at 3 yr.
- Caveat: low-tier journal, approximate numbers, no device given.

### doi:10.3390/dj12030055
- Taniguchi 2024 (Dent J, TMDU): retrospective case series, 36 sites in 20 patients, ridge preservation or augmentation with carbonated apatite granules alone (no membrane), with simultaneous (some immediate) or staged implants; CBCT pre-op and 3-6 mo for staged cases.
- Distant for us: carbonated apatite, not β-TCP; most simultaneous-case measures come from photographs. Regenerated bone height ~7 mm in preservation vs ~4.5 mm in augmentation sites, showing that a contained (intra-bony) defect regenerates far better than an uncontained one.
- Caveats: the β-TCP comparison ("regenerated bone less likely to be lost than β-TCP", "fewer granules than BBM at ~4 mo") is a clinical impression, not data. Granule loss in 7 RP cases after clot loss. Granule size printed only as "M size".

### doi:10.1186/s40902-023-00390-w
- Choi 2023 (Maxillofac Plast Reconstr Surg): retrospective, 25 patients grafted with pure β-TCP (Neo Bone, nanocrystal-coated) in cysts, sockets, sinuses and implant sites; panoramic radiographs at the first visit and 6 mo.
- Pure β-TCP, but measures are 2D and pool very different defects. Graft area kept 97.5±26.8% and relative density barely changed (1.15 -> 1.10) at 6 mo, so on radiographs a pure β-TCP fill looks volume-stable at 6 mo. The Discussion cites 75.6% area retention for conventional pure β-TCP, a useful comparator.
- Caveats: not CBCT (only one illustrated CBCT pair, Fig 4); product claims about the coating are uncritical; only 3 implant cases; no histology; granule size not given. Site counts in Table 5 (11 maxilla, 7 mandible) do not add up to 25.

### doi:10.4103/0972-124x.188335
- J Indian Soc Periodontol 2016 (authors not in the text file): randomized pilot, 15 patients with 3 sockets each: autogenous tooth graft vs β-TCP alloplast vs ungrafted, all under chorion membrane; CBCT on day 1 and at 4 mo.
- Direct β-TCP socket data with an empty-socket control in the same patients: 4-mo height loss 1.72 mm (β-TCP) vs 2.60 mm (empty), width loss 1.45 vs 2.29 mm. So β-TCP reduces but does not stop socket collapse at 4 mo. A good calibration pair for our socket-wall remodelling module.
- Caveats: pilot, tooth types mixed, CBCT device not given; β-TCP product ("SyboGraf-T Plug") form and particle size not stated; histology qualitative (β-TCP poorly integrated, immature bone at 4 mo).

### doi:10.1186/s12903-023-03647-y
- You 2023 (BMC Oral Health, Wuhan): retrospective, 58 ARP sites (atelocollagen plug vs Bio-Oss + Bio-Gide) in sockets with thin or slightly defective buccal walls; CBCT pre-extraction and at 6 mo, 3D superimposition with width at 0-8 mm levels, crest height and volume.

### doi:10.31080/asor.2021.04.0256
- Alesawy 2021 (Acta Sci Orthop, Cairo): RCT, 30 mandibular molar sockets: autogenous + ABB vs ABB vs gelatin sponge; CBCT post-op and 6 mo, core biopsy at 6 mo.

### doi:10.11606/t.25.2019.tde-04062019-150427
- PhD thesis (Univ. São Paulo, Bauru, 2019) with two articles on porous BCP 70% HA / 30% β-TCP (GenPhos-XP). Article 1: rat 8-mm calvarial defects, granules 0.7/1.0/1.5 mm vs autogenous bone, histomorphometric volumes at 4, 12, 24 wk. Article 2: human split-mouth sinus lift (12 patients), CBCT (i-CAT) at pre-op, 7 d and 4 mo, plus micro-CT (9.3 µm) and histology of 6-mo biopsies.

Granule size matters: 1.0 mm gave the most bone in rats, 1.5 mm the least.
- Caveats: thesis, may duplicate later journal papers; granule size in the human article (0.50-0.75 mm) differs from the rat one; internal inconsistency on the autograft volume loss (45% vs 24%); only 3 autograft biopsies.

### doi:10.3390/jcm10225231

- Longest β-TCP imaging series in the batch. ### doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-10268327/v1
- Zhang 2026 (Research Square preprint, Fuyang): retrospective, 61 patients / 80 anterior sites with horizontal defects, tenting screws + β-TCP/CGF composite with local autogenous chips and simultaneous implants, collagen + CGF membranes; CBCT pre-op, 0, 6, 12, 18 mo at 0/2/4 mm below the platform.

### doi:10.1038/s41598-021-93033-x

### doi:10.1155/2016/5406736

### doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0084932

### doi:10.23880/oajds-16000379
Lu 2023 case report: maxillary first molar socket with an 11 mm buccal dehiscence grafted with Bio-Oss and sealed with an ovate pontic; CBCT pre-extraction and 6 mo with linear and zonal volumetric measures; implant at 6 mo.

# Extraction notes, batch 05

### doi:10.1563/aaid-joi-d-11-00079
- Daif 2013 (J Oral Implantol): RCT, 28 patients, immediate implants in mandibular premolar sockets with Cerasorb M (pure beta-TCP, 500-1000 um) packed in gaps >2 mm vs no graft. Multislice CT at 3 and 6 mo after loading, HU density.
- The closest clinical match to our scenario in this batch (pure beta-TCP, immediate implant, premolar, gap >2 mm). Gives granule size, gap width, a two-stage loading timeline and HU at two time points in the same patients.
- Caveats: no post-op baseline scan, no voxel size, HU over a 3 mm ROI mixes residual ceramic with bone, so the higher HU in the TCP arm cannot be read as more bone. Only one image figure, not serial. Single author.

### doi:10.5005/jp-journals-10024-3158

- Useful as a validation target for buccal dimensions at 2/4/6 mm below crest and vertical crest-to-thread distance (Tables 4-6), and as an immediate-provisional loading protocol.

### doi:10.1007/s44445-025-00112-0

### doi:10.3390/bioengineering12080848

### doi:10.1111/clr.12041

### doi:10.1177/0885328206054167
- Nair, Luder, Maspero, Fischer, Schug 2006 (J Biomater Appl 20:307): pilot, 6 miniature pigs, mandibular third-incisor sockets filled with root replicas made of PLGA-coated beta-TCP granules (500-800 um) fused together; empty contralateral controls; light/electron microscopy and EDX at 20, 40, 60 wk.

### doi:10.5051/jpis.2015.45.5.162

### doi:10.3389/fvets.2022.859920

### doi:10.1186/s12903-026-09810-5

### doi:10.1186/s40729-021-00344-9

### doi:10.1111/cid.70079

### doi:10.4103/ccd.ccd_246_23

### doi:10.26650/eor.20241457793

- No own imaging, so no imaging row. - Caveats: not pure beta-TCP; decalcified sections; an empty control (E) is in the design figure but no results are reported; group sizes per material not stated; torque table labels units as N though text uses N cm.

### doi:10.1007/s13770-020-00277-1
- Beagle study (Ma 2020, Tissue Eng Regen Med): 12 dogs / 24 sinuses, sinus lift with simultaneous Straumann implants; graft = OSTEON Sinus with DMP1-transduced BMSCs, EGFP-BMSCs, plain BMSCs or scaffold alone (6 per group); explant CT, micro-CT and histology at 12 wk only (no serial imaging, no imaging row).

- Caveats: the scaffold is a beta-TCP coating on an HA scaffold (OSTEON Sinus), not pure beta-TCP; the methods text once calls the constructs "BMSC/Bio-Oss"; scaffold-only values are graph only; sinus model.

### doi:10.1016/j.oooo.2022.06.002
- Pilot human study (Montero 2023, OOOO): robocast 80% beta-TCP / 20% HA cylindrical bioblocks (2 x 8 mm, 60% porosity, 300-500 um macropores) placed into drilled sites next to implants in 12 edentulous patients, explanted by trephine at 10-16 wk for histology. No serial imaging, so no imaging row.

### doi:10.3390/dj14070445
- Single case report (Dent J 2026): immediate 3.8 x 13 mm implant in a maxillary lateral incisor socket with buccal dehiscence, with L-PRF membranes and a connective tissue graft but no bone substitute. CBCT at baseline (tooth present) and 12 mo, with bucco-palatal thickness at four levels.
- Useful as a "no graft" counterpart: ridge thickness increased by 1.4-3.2 mm at all levels in 12 mo. It also gives an L-PRF prep protocol. The palatal-plane reorientation method is worth copying for our own registration.
- Caveat: n = 1, the baseline is pre-extraction so it is not socket geometry, and there is no β-TCP. Authors are not in the text file.

### doi:10.1155/2022/5114332

- Gives 1-year buccal bone thickness at six levels (2.35-2.88 mm) next to a grafted 2 mm gap under immediate provisional loading. Usable as an end-state check only.

### doi:10.7759/cureus.117992
- Tanuja et al. 2026 (Cureus) case report: immediate 4 x 14 mm Dentium implant in tooth 11 with a deficient facial plate, gap and facial contour grafted with Bio-Oss under Bio-Gide, submerged for 3 mo. CBCT pre-op, at 12 mo and at 36 mo.
- The only number is ~2.2 mm facial bone 2 mm below the platform, unchanged from 1 to 3 years. That is a long-term plateau check, but the information content is low.
- Caveat: n = 1, no day-0 CBCT, xenograft.

### doi:10.4103/jpbs.jpbs_250_24
- Short paper (J Pharm Bioallied Sci 2024): 40 immediate implants, 20 with PRF in the socket and 20 without. CBCT "crestal bone" at four sides, at placement and at 12 mo.
- Gives a rough PRF effect: the decrease over 12 mo is about 0.4-0.5 mm with PRF and 0.8-1.0 mm without. Usable only as a weak qualitative growth-factor signal.

### doi:10.25258/ijddt.16.73s.140
- Jaiswal et al. 2026 (IJDDT): 20 patients with compromised sockets (buccal plate <1 mm, dehiscence or fenestration) got an immediate implant + GBR with FDBA or porcine xenograft under a collagen membrane. CBCT width at the implant crest at baseline, 6 and 9 mo.
- Width goes from 5.2 to 6.85 mm (FDBA) and 5.1 to 6.7 mm (porcine), with no difference between arms. This is a weak comparator for the gap-filled immediate implant, and there is no β-TCP.

### doi:10.5051/jpis.2404240212
- Prospective single-arm study (J Periodontal Implant Sci 2025, Seoul): 20 immediate implants (Warantec IU) in the maxillary esthetic zone, 17 analysed. Gaps and labial defects were grafted with Cerabone + collagen membrane when indicated. CBCT just after placement and at 450 d, superimposed.
- Good level-wise numbers: buccal bone thickness at 1/3/5 mm drops by 0.34-0.48 mm and bucco-lingual width by 0.63-0.78 mm over 15 mo. ISQ rises from 60 to 74-76 between day 10 and day 70. Useful as an implant-stability time course for early healing.
- Caveat: funded by the implant maker (Warantec). Grafted and ungrafted sites are pooled, the graft is bovine, and the CBCT device is not reported. The first author is not certain from the text.

### doi:10.1111/cid.13060
- Staas et al. 2022 (Clin Implant Dent Relat Res): prospective multicentre cohort of 100 flapless immediate implants with provisional crowns in maxillary incisors. Implant at least 2 mm palatal of the buccal plate, socket filled with Bio-Oss S (0.25-1 mm). CBCT superimposed pre-op, directly post-op and at 1 year (81 complete series).
- One of the best sets in the batch for validating the buccal side of the gap. The reconstructed buccal crest goes from 3.3 ± 1.2 mm at day 0 to 2.4 ± 1.1 mm at 1 year (−0.8 mm). Crest height goes from 3.1 to 1.7 mm. 7.5% lose the crest entirely. The gap width and non-occluding provisional match our loading scenario.
- Caveat: Bio-Oss (slowly resorbing), not β-TCP, so graft and new bone are measured as one crest. One level (implant shoulder) only. 7-year data are mentioned but not reported. No raw data.

### doi:10.3390/bioengineering9110649
- Retrospective technical report (Bioengineering 2022): 11 maxillary incisor implants (3 immediate, 8 delayed). Buccal dehiscences were grafted with Bio-Oss inside a resorbable membrane pouch fixed to periosteum tabs. CBCT (90 µm) at placement and after the final restoration.
- Per-case buccal bone thickness at the first thread is given. Mean augmented width 2.8 ± 0.6 mm loses 1.3 ± 0.8 mm. The three immediate cases go 2.0→1.2, 3.4→1.3 and 3.2→2.6 mm. A small extra check on buccal graft shrinkage.
- Caveat: xenograft, mostly delayed cases, variable follow-up, n = 11. Authors are not in the text file.

### doi:10.1111/jcpe.14135
- Park et al. 2025 (J Clin Periodontol), two-centre Korean RCT: 60 damaged non-molar sockets (mostly maxillary premolars) augmented with OSTEON 3 BCP (HA:β-TCP 60:40) particles or a press-fit cylindrical block, both under a collagen membrane. CBCT right after grafting and at 6 mo, with level-wise width H0-H5 and STL volumetry. Implant at 6 mo.
- The full level-wise table (Appendix 4) gives a 6-mo shrinkage profile of a CaP-grafted socket. With particles, the crest (H0) loses 1.9 mm (31%), dropping to ~0.3 mm at H5. Most loss is coronal, which is a direct shape check for our coronal resorption in the socket. Defect dimensions are also reported.
- Caveat: BCP 60:40, not pure β-TCP, and no implant in the graft phase. 10/30 blocks were exposed and removed, so attrition is high. Volume SDs differ between main text and appendix. Granule size is not given. Government-funded, industry product (Genoss).

### doi:10.4103/jpbs.jpbs_603_20
- Short RCT (J Pharm Bioallied Sci 2021): 40 single-rooted sockets, β-TCP socket preservation vs empty healing. CBCT at baseline and 14 wk, buccal/lingual plate height, and baseline plate thickness.
- Relevant scenario (pure β-TCP socket fill, human). Median facial-plate change 0.61 mm with β-TCP vs 1.17 mm in controls over 14 wk. Baseline buccal plate thickness 0.5-0.9 mm and BL width ~8.2 mm feed the socket geometry.

### doi:10.54589/aol.34/2/119

- Gives 4-mo socket histomorphometry: bone 33-34%, residual DBBG 5-8%, soft tissue ~60%. That is a mid-healing composition point for a grafted socket.

### doi:10.3390/biomedicines12112537

### doi:10.2147/ijn.s481498
- Feng et al. 2024 (Int J Nanomedicine): rabbit mandibular incisor sockets (n = 5 per group) left empty, filled with gelatin sponge, or gelatin sponge + simvastatin (0.5 mg) or simvastatin nanomicelles. In vivo CBCT at 0, 2 and 4 wk, histology at 4 wk.
- Early-healing HU curve of an empty socket (416→522 HU at 2→4 wk) and ridge-length resorption (11.9%→15.8%). A drug that speeds up mineralisation roughly doubles HU. This is useful only as a rough early-healing check on timing.
- Caveat: rabbit incisor socket, no implant or ceramic. SDs are in graphs only. Simvastatin is not one of our growth factors. Release kinetics were not measured.

### doi:10.1186/s40729-023-00501-2
- Takafuji et al. 2024 (Int J Implant Dent), prospective: lateral sinus lift with a pure β-TCP plate (10 mm x 2 mm, Brain Base) supported by implants or screws, with only clot underneath. CBCT pre-op and at 1, 2 and 5 years, segmented in 3D (STL) for augmented volume and plate volume.
- Rare long-term pure β-TCP resorption data in humans. Relative to the 1-year volume, the plate is 52% resorbed at 2 years (18-88%) and 83% at 5 years (41-100%). This shows slow multi-year β-TCP clearance in a dense, poorly perfused site and helps set the slow end of our resorption rate. Augmented volume shrinks only 15-22% after year 1 (per-patient values in Table 2). Graft and bone were segmented separately.
- Caveat: sinus, not a socket. The plate is a dense shape, not granules, and porosity is not given. There is no day-0 scan, so year-1 resorption is unknown. Patient count differs (8 in results, 9 in abstract). No raw data.

### doi:10.5966/sctm.2014-0039
- Rajan et al. 2014 (Stem Cells Transl Med): in vitro optimisation of seeding expanded bone marrow cells (ixmyelocel-T) onto Cerasorb β-TCP particles (500-1000 µm), plus one patient whose 3 mm-wide anterior maxillary ridge was reconstructed with the cell-loaded β-TCP. CBCT pre-op, immediately post-graft and at 4 mo, with a biopsy at implant placement.
- Gives a pure β-TCP number in humans: 80% of the β-TCP matrix resorbed in the 4-mo biopsy, and the grafted width shrank 25% by 4 mo. Both are fast compared with sinus or plate data, which is useful when bounding the resorption rate. The seeding numbers (60% at 15 min, 81% at 30 min, 88-94% survival) and cell dose (~10^7 cells) belong in the cell-delivery parameters.

### doi:10.12659/msm.944713
- Wang et al. 2025 (Med Sci Monit) RCT: 32 implants with simultaneous GBR of buccal defects (mostly molars, not immediate). Bio-Oss granules under a gelatin/PLA or Guidor membrane. CBCT pre-op, on the day of surgery and at 6 mo, with Mimics Boolean volumetry by a blinded third party.
- Clean volumetric numbers for particulate graft loss: 350-391 mm³ at day 0, ~43-49% lost by 6 mo. That is the scale of particle collapse and displacement for a non-contained buccal graft (DBBM barely resorbs, so loss is mostly mechanical). Useful as an upper bound of non-resorptive volume loss.

### doi:10.3390/ijms25020862

### doi:10.3390/jfb17020086

### doi:10.1007/s10856-023-06734-2

### doi:10.3390/jfb17010006
- RCT (J Funct Biomater 2025, Brazil): 60 anterior maxillary single-rooted sockets in 15 patients, four arms (empty, autograft, L-PRF, L-PRF + HA/β-TCP 150-425 µm sticky bone). Core biopsies at implant placement at 8 wk. No imaging.

### doi:10.5966/sctm.2015-0369
- Prins et al. 2016 (Stem Cells Transl Med), phase I: 10 sinus lifts with Ceros β-TCP (0.7-1.4 mm, 60% porosity) or BoneCeramic BCP (60/40, 0.5-1.0 mm, 90% porosity), seeded or not with freshly isolated adipose SVF (20 x 10^6 cells per 2 g, 30 min). 6-mo biopsies examined by micro-CT (10 µm, graft/bone separated by mineralisation threshold plus "onion peeling") and histomorphometry.
- Pure β-TCP at 6 mo: residual graft GV/TV 29.6% by histology (10.6% by micro-CT) without cells and 17.4% with SVF. BV/TV is 12% vs 16.4%. So cells speed up both bone formation and β-TCP removal.
